Safety

Every message is checked for crisis risk before anything else. Three independent layers run, and the highest risk wins. A missed crisis is the costliest error, so the threshold leans towards recall; the crisis banner stays reachable at all times because no detector catches every case.

Intent

Each message is classified into one of seven intents so the reply can match what the person is asking for: to vent, to get advice, or to get information.

Knowledge retrieval

For questions, Lumina grounds its reply in passages from NHS, NIMH, Mayo Clinic and HelpGuide. Passages are used only when they are close enough to the message.

Market

Eight competing apps, compared on what their store pages show, what their vendors claim, and what their users write in reviews. The findings come first; the evidence for each follows.

Findings and product requirements

  1. F1Pricing is the leading complaint for Replika

    Evidence
    About 40% of Replika's newest reviews complain about pricing; Woebot and Wysa are around 6–7% (Review themes). Every app except Woebot sells in-app purchases (App facts).
    Opportunity
    Make free core support a differentiator.
    Requirement
    The core support conversation and crisis access are always free. No feature starts or stops mid-conversation because of payment.

  2. F2Access barriers hurt Woebot

    Evidence
    About 20% of Woebot's newest reviews raise access barriers, mostly access codes and lock-outs; Replika is at about 16%, mostly account and age-gate locks (Review themes). Woebot is sold only in the US storefront and was last updated in Dec 2025; Youper is absent from all four English storefronts (App facts).
    Opportunity
    There is a gap for open, English-language AI support with no eligibility gate.
    Requirement
    No access codes and no employer or insurer gating. Onboarding is a single step.

  3. F3Conversation quality is a frequent complaint

    Evidence
    About 30% for Replika and about 12% each for Wysa and Woebot (Review themes). The featured reviews describe scripted, repetitive or misunderstood replies.
    Opportunity
    A conversational, context-aware assistant, which competitors' scripted flows are not.
    Requirement
    LLM conversation that uses the recent turns, evaluated for relevance and consistency.

  4. F4Emotional support is what satisfied users praise

    Evidence
    About 67% of Wysa's newest reviews and about 45% of Woebot's praise emotional support (Review themes). Positive reviews also centre on support and therapeutic tools (Segmentation).
    Opportunity
    Empathy is the core value, not the feature count.
    Requirement
    Empathy-first replies, evaluated with a rubric. Therapeutic exercises are offered as optional tools.

  5. F5Most competitors link data to the user

    Evidence
    Of the 7 apps with store pages, 5 declare "data linked to you" or "used to track you". Only Wysa and Ash declare "not linked" (Benchmark, privacy_label).
    Opportunity
    Private-by-design processing — local LLM, local safety models and local voice, no message logging.
    Requirement
    Keep processing local by default, never log message text, and state plainly what is stored.

  6. F6Accessibility is rarely declared

    Evidence
    Only Replika declares accessibility features (Benchmark, accessibility_declared).
    Opportunity
    Accessibility and voice as a differentiator.
    Requirement
    Natural voice input and output, plus WCAG basics such as labels, focus handling and live regions.

  7. F7Crisis features are rare

    Evidence
    Of the apps with store pages, only Earkick's vendor text claims a crisis or SOS feature (Benchmark, crisis_features).
    Opportunity
    A visible, evaluated crisis layer is a differentiator.
    Requirement
    Crisis help is always one tap away. Risk detection runs before every reply, as built and measured in the Safety section.

Segmentation

Clustering the review evidence gave no robust need-based segments. Text clusters show almost no structure (silhouette ≈ 0.05). Theme-profile clusters are stable, but they mostly separate satisfied reviewers from dissatisfied ones. The codebook themes are therefore used as need categories: emotional support, conversation quality, therapeutic tools, pricing, access, privacy, safety and human support. They are not demographic segments, and the data supports no claims about shares of users.
